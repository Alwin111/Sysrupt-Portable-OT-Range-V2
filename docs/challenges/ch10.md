# Challenge 10 — Device Takeover

**Points:** 100 · **Difficulty:** Hard · **Type:** Defensive · **Surface:** PROFINET DCP (saved evidence)

> Spoiler-free. This guide explains the *surface* and the *idea* — not the answer.

## The Story

On a PROFINET network, devices announce themselves by name using **DCP** (Discovery and Configuration
Protocol). An attacker added a rogue device claiming a legitimate station's name. Your job is forensic:
from a saved capture, tell the real device from the impostor.

## Where to start

- The **saved DCP capture is an evidence attachment** — download it from IR webmail
  (`http://10.0.1.1/mail`); attachments are real files.
- Cross-reference with the **Engineering Asset Inventory** and **OUI registry** at
  `http://10.0.1.1/inventory`.
- Submit your answer on the **Record Findings** page (`http://10.0.1.1/investigate`).

## Objectives

1. Open the saved DCP capture.
2. Find a station **name claimed by two different MAC addresses**.
3. Check each MAC against the inventory and OUI registry.
4. Submit the impostor's MAC.

## Hints

<details><summary>1. What am I looking for in the capture?</summary>

Two frames announcing the *same* station name from *different* source MACs. One is the real device; the
other is the impostor.
</details>

<details><summary>2. Which of the two is fake?</summary>

A MAC's first three octets (its OUI) identify the vendor. Compare each MAC against the inventory's known
device and the OUI registry — the impostor's prefix won't match the vendor it's pretending to be.
</details>

## What you'll learn

- How PROFINET DCP name-announcement works and how it's abused.
- How to use OUI/vendor data to spot a spoofed device.
- Basic network forensics from a saved capture.

## Scoreboard

Awarded when the impostor's MAC is submitted correctly.
