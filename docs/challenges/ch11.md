# Challenge 11 — Stabilise the Reactor

**Points:** 300 · **Difficulty:** Expert · **Surface:** Modbus/TCP (TCP 502) · **Toolkit:** `sysrupt modbus`

> Spoiler-free. This guide explains the *surface* and the *idea* — not the answer.

## The Story

The reactor is drifting out of its safe operating band. You have the operator **HMI** showing live
values, and a **Modbus** control channel underneath it. Map what you see on the screen to the registers
that drive it, and hold the plant in its all-safe condition.

## Where to start

- The reactor control is **Modbus/TCP on 502**; drive it with `./bin/sysrupt help modbus`.
- The operator HMI is at `http://10.0.1.1/hmi` — watch the gauges (core temp, pressure, neutron flux,
  safe band) move as you act.
- **`robots.txt`** points to a control page whose notice explains the setup.

## Objectives

1. Follow `robots.txt` to the control page and read the notice.
2. Map HMI values (temp, pressure, flux, …) to Modbus registers.
3. Bring every reading into its safe band.
4. **Hold** the all-safe condition for the required time.

## Hints

<details><summary>1. How do I find which register is which?</summary>

Read the Modbus registers and watch the HMI at the same time — a value that moves on the screen when a
register changes is your mapping. The control page notice helps you orient.
</details>

<details><summary>2. I hit "safe" but it didn't solve.</summary>

The challenge needs *all* conditions safe *at once*, held for several seconds — not a momentary touch.
Think about stabilising, not just nudging one value.
</details>

<details><summary>3. Values keep drifting back.</summary>

The simulated plant is dynamic. The toolkit's verification ladder shows observed state change — use it to
see whether you're holding the band or just grazing it.
</details>

## What you'll learn

- How HMI values map to raw Modbus registers.
- How to read and write Modbus holding registers.
- Why process control is about *holding* a state, not a single write.

## Scoreboard

Awarded when all readings are in the safe band and held.
