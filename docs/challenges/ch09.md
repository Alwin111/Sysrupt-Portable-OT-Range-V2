# Challenge 09 — Trojan Historian

**Points:** 150 · **Difficulty:** Medium · **Type:** Defensive · **Surface:** Web (`/mail`, `/investigate`)

> Spoiler-free. This guide explains the *surface* and the *idea* — not the answer.

## The Story

Someone tampered with the historian: a record was quietly deleted to hide activity. Classic
anti-forensics. Your job is to spot the gap in the trend, recover the missing value from the retained
backup, and restore it.

## Where to start

- Read the IR webmail thread about the trend (`http://10.0.1.1/mail`) — it points at a `TURBINE.VIB`
  trend gap.
- The relevant **backup is an evidence attachment** (a downloadable file) on that thread.
- Restore via the form on the **Record Findings** page (`http://10.0.1.1/investigate`).

## Objectives

1. Spot the gap in the trend (a missing sample).
2. Read the backup to find the deleted sample's sequence and value.
3. Restore it via the submit form.
4. Keep it stable.

## Hints

<details><summary>1. How do I know which record is missing?</summary>

The backup trend shows every sample; the live trend is missing one (shown as a gap). Line them up — the
sequence number that's present in the backup but absent live is your target.
</details>

<details><summary>2. What do I submit?</summary>

The restore needs both the **sequence number** and the **value** of the deleted sample, read straight
from the backup file. Submit them together.
</details>

<details><summary>3. It says "restored" but not solved yet.</summary>

The challenge wants the record to *stay* restored for a short hold. Re-submit the same values after the
hold completes to see it marked solved.
</details>

## What you'll learn

- How deleted records show up as trend gaps.
- Why retained backups are essential for integrity recovery.
- The anti-forensics mindset of hiding activity by deleting data.

## Scoreboard

Awarded when the deleted record is restored and held stable.
